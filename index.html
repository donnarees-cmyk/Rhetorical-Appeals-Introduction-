What Are Rhetorical Appeals?

Rhetorical appeals are the tools writers and speakers use to persuade an audience. There are three main types — each one targets a different part of how we think and feel.

🤝
Ethos
Credibility & Trust

Ethos is about trust. When a writer uses ethos, they're saying: "You should believe me because I'm qualified, experienced, or honest." It answers the question: Why should I listen to this person?

Kid-Friendly Analogy

Imagine your basketball coach gives you tips on shooting form. You trust the advice because the coach has played and taught the game for years. That trust? That's ethos.

Clue words to look for:

expert studies show according to years of experience researcher as a...

"As a veterinarian with 15 years of experience, I can assure you that adopting a shelter dog is one of the most rewarding decisions a family can make."

The speaker builds trust by mentioning their job (veterinarian) and experience (15 years). That credibility makes you more likely to believe them.

📊
Logos
Logic & Evidence

Logos is about logic. When a writer uses logos, they back up their argument with facts, statistics, data, or clear reasoning. It answers the question: What evidence supports this?

Kid-Friendly Analogy

Your school cafeteria posts a graph showing that food waste dropped 40% after starting a "share table." The numbers prove it worked — that's logos in action.

Clue words to look for:

data percent statistics study found numbers evidence results

"A recent study of 2,000 students found that those who read for 20 minutes a day scored 30% higher on vocabulary tests than students who didn't."

The writer uses a specific study, a large sample size (2,000 students), and a clear statistic (30% higher). These facts and numbers make the argument hard to argue with.

❤️
Pathos
Emotion & Feelings

Pathos is about emotion. When a writer uses pathos, they make you feel something — sad, happy, angry, or inspired — to persuade you. It answers the question: How does this make me feel?

Kid-Friendly Analogy

You see a poster of a lonely puppy behind cage bars with the words "Give me a home." The sad image makes you want to help — that emotional pull is pathos.

Clue words to look for:

heartbreaking imagine devastating heartwarming fear love tragic

"Every night, thousands of children go to bed hungry, wondering if they'll eat tomorrow. You can change that with just one dollar a day."

The writer paints a vivid, emotional picture of hungry children. The goal is to make you feel compassion — and that feeling motivates you to act.

Quick Reference Guide

Appeal Means Asks Uses
Ethos Credibility Can I trust you? Expertise, titles, experience
Logos Logic Does this make sense? Facts, data, statistics
Pathos Emotion How does this feel? Stories, vivid words, images
